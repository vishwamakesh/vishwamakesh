UNIT 1: WRITING PROCESS

Blog

When I first started thinking about a phenomenon that I could use for this project, I kept thinking about the experience of becoming more independent at college while also missing the convenience and comfort at home. At first, when I thought about independence I was mostly thinking about freedom. Being from Virginia, moving away from home to college meant being able to be on my own schedule, deciding what I wanted to do, and having more control over my day-to-day actions. However, now being a sophomore in college, I realized that the concept of having independence is a lot more than having more freedom. It meant that I lost the usual routines I had at home all my life, and forms of support that I did not notice or appreciate while I was living at home. One of the strongest patterns I have noticed is that I can enjoy being independent and having more freedom, but still the comfortability of home. During the day, college can feel like a rush of excitement, because I have my own responsibilities, my own class schedule, and the ability to see my friends whenever I want. At other times, when I feel stressed or tired, I notice how I miss the small things about being home. These could be simple things like eating meals with my family, being around people I’ve been with my whole life, having certain snacks available to me at all times, or just being in a place I have called home for the last 19 years of my life. My current interpretation is that moving away for college forces students to create a new form of “home” for themselves. Being independent for the first time can be exciting, but it also requires the ability to adapt to your environment, create comfort within it, and stay stable. At the same time. I know I might be assuming how college students experience being independent is similar to the way I did. Some students might feel like they are less homesick than others may miss home more strongly. This leaves me with several questions. Why can freedom and independence feel excited and lonely at the same time? Why do family routines feel more important after leaving them? How do college students bring the sense of comfort that you get at home to college with them? Three popular culture artifacts that could help me explore deeply into this phenomenon are college move-in TikTok's, scenes from movies of shows that show the characters moving away from their families, or any short-form content about college home sicknesses. My strongest thing I could use would probably be the college move-in TikToks because they focus on how excited they are to decorate their dorm or apartment, meeting new people for the first time, and finally having freedom. Looking at my phenomenon through these videos could create a different lens on this topic since social media tends to only show the good parts of all of these. These videos may leave out the sense of loneliness, stress, or discomfort that is often associated with after moving in and finally settling into college. While moving away to college can seem like a path towards freedom and independence, looking into the idea of missing home suggests that being “free” also involves adapting, rebuilding comfort, and belonging in your new environment.
